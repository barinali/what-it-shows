The three problems

Parser reads month first. The shared parser reads every date month first. Tenants that write the day first get flipped dates: 7 April comes out as 4 July. This is the bug #4471 was opened for. The desk files a few reports of the third problem here too, because it counts by tenant (the table says how many).

Booking-v3 hint. Tenants on booking-v3 write the day first, but the form prints MM/DD/YYYY under the date field. Their people type what the hint says, and the form reads it the tenant’s way. It is the same flip with a different cause, and it only happens through the form channel. Fixing the parser did not touch it. Engineering will not fix it: the booking-v3 form is retired.

One day off. The desk’s own notes list these reports. Their text has no flipped month: a Tuesday shows as a Monday, one client in Lisbon and another in Toronto got different days for the same meeting. That points to time zones at a day boundary, not to date order. The engineering update names no fix for it. It needs an engineer who owns scheduling and time zones.

Why they were merged

Decision 0001 merged on the subject line alone, and decision 0002 replaces it with the cause. Subjects such as “Wrong day on the invite” and “Month and day swapped?” appear in all three problems, so the match could not tell them apart. Intake’s tag was a guess from the reporter’s browser, and it is wrong for part of the queue (see the table). The tenant tells the cause, not the subject and not the browser.
